Google Universal Cart: one shopping cart across Search and Gemini

What Google's new cart does, how checkout works, and the catches (U.S. only, Wallet cards).

On Oct 15, 2026 Google launched Universal Cart, a shopping cart that works across Google Search and the Gemini app. Google says it is rolling out in the U.S. and calls it a more organized alternative to tabs and screenshots, especially for holiday shopping. Here is what Google says it does, and what to know first.

🛒 Four things it does

  1. One cart for many stores. Save products from multiple retailers in one cart, wherever you shop on Google, and your progress is saved across Google. Google's example: a handbag from Steve Madden saved in AI Mode in Search, then a sweater from Quince and lip gloss from Ulta Beauty added in the Gemini app. All of it is there when you come back.
  2. Price-drop and back-in-stock alerts. It works in the background and sends you notifications. It is built on Google's Shopping Graph, which Google says holds more than 60 billion continually refreshed product listings.
  3. Card perks and promo codes. It is built on Google Wallet, so Google says it understands your card perks and helps you choose between payment methods. It also shows relevant promo codes in your cart.
  4. Checkout. Pay with Google Pay in just a few taps with many stores right in the cart, or transfer your items to the merchant's own cart and finish on their site. Google names Nike, Sephora, Target, Ulta Beauty, Walmart, Wayfair and many Shopify merchants. The merchant stays the seller of record, so order confirmations and customer support come from the merchant.

⚠️ The catch

  • U.S. only for now. Google says it is rolling out in the U.S.; no other countries are mentioned.
  • In-cart checkout works with "many" stores, not all. Otherwise you are sent to the store's own site to finish.
  • It uses your Google Wallet cards. The card-perk suggestions are built on Google Wallet.
  • The savings claims are Google's own description. There is no independent test yet.

Setup

Google's announcement does not list step-by-step setup instructions, so none are given here. See Google's post below for the latest details.
