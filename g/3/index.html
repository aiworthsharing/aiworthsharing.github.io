How to tell Facebook's AI what you want to see

Use Facebook's new "Your algorithm" to fix your Feed and Reels in a few taps. No tech skills needed.

Meta just added Your algorithm to Facebook. Instead of guessing what you like from your clicks, Facebook now lets you tell it, in your own words, what you want to see more or less of in Feed and Reels. Meta says your recommendations respond right away. Here's how to use it.

✅ Before you start

  • Meta announced the feature on Oct 13, 2026 and says it is rolling out. Meta hasn't published a schedule, so you may not see it yet.
  • You use Facebook (Feed and Reels). No paid plan is mentioned in the announcement.

📱 Step by step

  1. Open Facebook and find any post in Feed or Reels.
  2. Tap the three-dot menu on the post. Meta updated this menu to make the new tools easier to reach.
  3. Choose "Your algorithm". This is a dedicated space where you add and manage the topics you want to see in Feed and Reels.
  4. Add the topics you want to see more of, and the ones you want to see less of, in your own words.

👍 Quick feedback on any post

  1. On a post in Feed or Reels, tap Interested or Not interested.
  2. Facebook may ask a follow-up question about what you liked or didn't like.
  3. Answer in plain English. Meta says your recommendations update in real time, even within the same session.

🧠 What's new under the hood

Facebook already learns from what you like, comment on and watch (among thousands of other signals). Meta says large language models (the same kind of AI behind chatbots) now let it understand the meaning behind your words too, and combine that with everything it already knows about what you enjoy.

⚠️ Good to know

  • It adds to the usual ranking system, it doesn't replace it.
  • Meta hasn't said how much weight your words get compared with your activity.
  • If you don't see "Your algorithm" in the menu yet, it may not have reached your account yet. Meta says it is rolling out but hasn't shared dates.

Sources

Meta Newsroom, "Your Algorithm, Your Words: New Ways to Shape What You See on Facebook" (Oct 13, 2026). Menu wording may differ slightly by device and app version.
