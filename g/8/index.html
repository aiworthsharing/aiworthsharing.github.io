Apple's tougher App Store rules on AI explicit images

What Apple changed on Oct 16, what it means for you, and the catches.

On Oct 16, 2026 Apple announced stronger App Review Guidelines, the rules every app must follow to be on the App Store. Apple says pornographic material, including apps that create sexually explicit images of real people without their consent ("nudification" apps), has long been prohibited. The update strengthens and extends those protections. Here is what changed, based on Apple's own post.

🛡️ Three things that changed

  1. Intimate images made without consent must be stopped and removed (rule 1.2). Apps must proactively prevent and remove this kind of content, and Apple now says so explicitly for images generated using AI tools. Developers who don't put adequate safeguards in place, or don't fix violations after notice, risk being removed from the App Store.
  2. No bait-and-switch (rule 2.5.19). Apple clarifies that apps can't misrepresent what they do during App Review and then change after approval, for example by adding features that were not in the version Apple reviewed. Apps doing this can be removed from the App Store and from the Apple Developer Program.
  3. Stricter wording on the most serious content (Section 5). Apple says apps that facilitate human trafficking or the exploitation of children are removed from the App Store, and the updated wording makes clear they are also blocked from alternative distribution.

📊 Apple's numbers

Apple says that last year it prevented over 150,000 apps with safety issues from reaching customers, and terminated over 193,000 developer accounts for fraud and abuse.

⚠️ The catch

  • It builds on rules Apple already had. Apple calls the ban on this kind of content long-held; the update clarifies and extends it.
  • Bad actors hide features until after approval. Apple says violations trace back to malicious actors, in some cases coordinated fraud operations, who conceal harmful content that only becomes active once an app is approved.
  • These are App Store rules. They govern apps on the App Store; Apple's post does not talk about websites or other places.
  • The numbers above are Apple's own, with no independent check.

What you can do

Apple's post is addressed to developers and does not list steps for users, so none are given here. See Apple's post and the guidelines below for the full wording.

Source

Apple Developer News, "Strengthening App Review Guidelines against nonconsensual intimate imagery and bait-and-switch apps", Oct 16, 2026: developer.apple.com/news. Guidelines: developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines
